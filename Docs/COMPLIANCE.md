# OpsForge Compliance Framework

OpsForge enforces strict compliance standards to ensure legal readiness, forensic integrity, and contributor clarity.  
This document consolidates U.S. policies, global standards, and chain-of-custody requirements.

---

## ⚖️ Compliance Overview
OpsForge is structured as a modular ecosystem with strict boundaries:
- **BuildMate** → software-only (construction SaaS workflows, compliance logging, project ops).  
- **DroneOps** → all drone hardware and aerial intelligence (surveying, surveillance, counter‑drone, telemetry).  
- **SmartSecurity** → centralized surveillance and orchestration.  
- **DeepForensics** → forensic attribution, evidence preservation, chain‑of‑custody.  
- **NetworkAudit** → IT/network monitoring, diagnostics, and compliance.  

Compliance ensures contributors respect these boundaries and maintain readiness across all modules.

---

## 🇺🇸 U.S. Compliance
OpsForge aligns with U.S. federal and state standards:
- **FERPA** → Educational data privacy.  
- **HIPAA** → Healthcare data protection.  
- **CJIS** → Criminal justice information security.  
- **FAA** → DroneOps compliance for aerial intelligence hardware.  
- **NIST/ISO** → Secure coding and audit standards.  

---

## 🌍 Global Compliance
OpsForge aligns with international standards:
- **GDPR** → European data protection.  
- **ISO/IEC 27001** → Information security management.  
- **ISO/IEC 27037** → Digital evidence handling.  
- **ICAO** → DroneOps compliance for international aerial intelligence.  
- **UN Cybercrime Convention** → Global forensic readiness.  

---

## 🔗 Chain of Custody
OpsForge enforces strict forensic attribution and evidence preservation:
- Immutable audit trails for all modules.  
- DeepForensics ensures provenance and attribution.  
- Chain-of-custody documentation maintained in [Docs/ChainOfCustody.md](Docs/ChainOfCustody.md).  
- BuildMate logs compliance workflows (software-only).  
- DroneOps logs aerial telemetry and hardware events.  

---

## 🚫 Prohibited Actions
- Unauthorized commercial use of OpsForge modules.  
- Mixing drone hardware references into BuildMate (software-only).  
- Circumventing CI/CD validation or audit logging.  
- Public disclosure of unpatched vulnerabilities.
